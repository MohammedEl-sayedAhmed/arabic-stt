# Arabic–English meeting transcription — findings (2026-09-24 → 25)

## In short

The goal: transcribe recorded meetings and calls in **Egyptian Arabic with English tech terms**, with
**Speaker 1 / Speaker 2** labels told apart by voice, free to try, keeping audio private. The handoff
asked whether **R2T2** could do it instead of ElevenLabs.

- **R2T2: no.** It runs on a CPU, but it is among the least accurate models tested (47.1% WER on
  Egyptian tech speech, whole clip, vs 15.7% for Cohere measured the same way), no better than its
  own base model, and it writes English terms in Arabic letters.
- **Best local models:** **Cohere Transcribe Arabic** (13.4% WER on the public Egyptian set, by far
  the fewest errors on Arabic words, ~0.3x real time on meetings) and the **whisper-medium
  Arabic–English code-switching fine-tune** (18.5%, keeps ~90% of English terms in English, ~0.5x
  real time on meetings). The WER gap between them is not statistically proven.
- **On your real meetings both are far behind ElevenLabs**: they disagree with its transcripts on
  **44–49% of the words** — mostly by dropping or Arabizing English terms and dropping fast
  exchanges. For minutes you rely on, keep using ElevenLabs (as Mojaz already does, with output
  language English); use the local tools for private or offline drafts.
- **Speaker labels by voice work locally**: with the TitaNet-small voiceprint model (now the default)
  **11–12% of words** go to the wrong person on real meetings — 8–9% pooled over the five
  2–3-person meetings (2–11% on four of them, 17% on a 2-person one where ElevenLabs' own labels are
  27% off); ElevenLabs' own labels overall: ~6% — and the speaker count is estimated correctly in 5
  of 6 meetings.
- A working **proof of concept** does all of this offline:

```sh
.venv/bin/python transcribe.py path/to/call.wav --speakers 2      # or --speakers 0 to estimate
```

- **The app, Tafrigh** (`./app.sh`): the same in a browser tab on this laptop — pick any of the five
  models (three local, ElevenLabs and Speechmatics with your own key), follow the progress, then play
  back, search, correct and export the transcript. See [the app](09-app.md).

## Key numbers

| | Public Egyptian set (Perle, 16 kHz) | Your meetings (vs ElevenLabs) | English kept (Perle / meetings) | Speed on this CPU (× real time: meetings / Perle clips) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cohere Transcribe Arabic | **13.4%** | **43.8%** (with speaker labels) | 77% / 46% | **0.26 / 0.36** |
| whisper-medium code-switching (default) | 18.5% | 48.8% | **90% / 57%** | 0.53 / 1.08 |
| Whisper large-v3 + style hint | 20.8% | — | 88% / — | — / 2.15 |
| R2T2 | 47.1% *(whole clip)* | — | 25% / — | — / 0.82 *(whole clip)* |

WER = word error rate (lower is better). "vs ElevenLabs" = share of words that differ from the
ElevenLabs reference transcript, which is itself machine output. Speed: processing time ÷ audio
length; the meeting figures are 10-minute excerpts through `transcribe.py` with speaker labels,
pauses included; the Perle figures are 4–12-second clips, where the fixed cost per chunk weighs
more.
